## Assets and actors

- **Asset:** funds, ERC-20 tokens and ERC-721 / ERC-1155 NFTs held at the EVM address derived for each vault NFT.
- **Owner:** the current holder of the NFT. **Seller / buyer:** the old and new owner around a sale.
- **Guardian:** one NEAR account per vault that can veto a queued transaction, a looser policy change or a sale.
- **Agent (`proposer`):** an optional NEAR account named in the policy, typically run by an AI model, that can propose and sign transactions within the policy. It is assumed it can be fooled (prompt injection) or fully compromised.
- **MPC network:** the NEAR Chain Signatures signers (8 nodes according to the NEAR documentation).

## Core rule: validate what you sign

The contract stores the transaction fields (chain id, nonce, fees, gas limit, destination, value, data), checks the policy on those fields and computes the EIP-1559 signing hash itself. A caller never supplies a hash. Only five calldata shapes are ever built: a plain ETH transfer, ERC-20 `transfer` and `approve`, and ERC-721 / ERC-1155 `safeTransferFrom` whose `from` is the vault itself (at most 196 bytes of calldata). Message signing (permits, EIP-712) and EIP-7702 authorizations are never produced.

## Threats

| # | Threat | Planned mitigation | State |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Pre-signed transaction.** The seller obtains a valid signature, sells the NFT and broadcasts the transaction afterwards | Two-phase sale: `start_sale` freezes new signatures; the buyer audits the external-chain nonce against the contract's `next_nonce`; if a signed transaction is outstanding, a cancel transaction with the same nonce is signed; only then does `nft_transfer` succeed | Mitigated, with a residual: during a sale the vault signs only cancellations; `scripts/sale-audit.mjs` compares the external nonce with the contract's next nonce and lists every kept signature; `cancel_nonce` makes it unminable. Proven on Sepolia (vault 41: NOT SAFE → cancellation mined → SAFE → transfer → kept payment rejected). Residual: the contract cannot read Ethereum, so the buyer must run the audit and pay through an escrow that binds the transfer to the audited sale (threat 23) |
| 2 | The seller adds themself as an allowed destination and drains the cap before selling | Every change of owner deletes the policy, its pending change and its spending windows; the daily cap bounds the damage | Partial: policy and queue reset tested (unit + testnet) |
| 3 | Misreading of calldata lets a dangerous approval through | Hash is built from validated fields; golden tests compare the Rust hash with viem for the same fields | Partial: the hash matches viem on 19 vectors, the strict classifier and the policy are unit-tested, and `check_transaction` refuses bad approvals on testnet. Live: a WETH `approve` and a WETH `transfer` were signed by the contract and mined on Sepolia (M10). Approvals count against the token's daily cap (M12, R12-3: before, repeated approvals to an allowed spender could exceed it), with a unit test, a negative control and an attack in the suite |
| 4 | A bug makes the contract sign something it should not | A single signing path; an attack suite that tries each bypass; manual review | Partial: one signing path (`execute_proposal`), which only signs the hash it computed from a proposal that passed the policy; the callback also checks the MPC signature against the vault key with `ecrecover`. The attack suite (44 attacks, `sandbox-tests/tests/attacks.rs`) tries the private callbacks and every policy bypass |
| 5 | The `sign` callback sees a different owner than the one who requested it | Each proposal records the owner epoch; transfers are blocked while a signature is in flight | Mitigated: while a signature is in flight the NFT cannot be transferred and no second signature can start (unit tests; sandbox test `a_signature_in_flight_locks_the_vault`, where the mock signer holds its answer with yield/resume like the MPC network); proposals also carry the owner epoch |
| 6 | Race on the daily cap | Charge the caps when a proposal is committed for signing, in one synchronous step with the nonce; give it back only if the answer provably cannot spend (wrong key). Windows are fixed 24 h: across a window boundary up to 2x the cap can be signed (declared) | Partial: caps are charged when a proposal is committed for signing, in one synchronous step with the nonce (review P1, reproduced and fixed with a unit test); live on Sepolia since M7. Since M12 approvals are charged to the token cap too |
| 7 | Permissions the seller left on the external chain: an ERC-20 approval that was already mined survives a sale and leaves no pending signature | Only approvals to allowed spenders up to the token's `max_approval` can be signed; amounts above 128 bits are refused, so 2^256-1 and 2^255 both fail. The buyer's audit finds every `approve` in the vault's signed history and reads the allowance left on the token; any allowance above 0 makes it NOT SAFE (the seller revokes with `approve(spender, 0)` before the sale) | Mitigated with the audit. Live: a WETH approval to the seller was signed and mined on Sepolia; with every nonce mined the audit said NOT SAFE; after the revocation it said SAFE. Since M12 the audit also scans the vault's Approval events on every token its policy names or its history touched, and fails if an uncertain nonce was mined (something the contract never recorded). Residual: the buyer must run the audit (the contract cannot read Ethereum), and an approval on a token outside the policy and the history is not scanned |
| 8 | Trust in the MPC network | Not solved; declared. The contract does check that every signature it stores recovers to the vault key, so a wrong or tampered answer is rejected and undone (unit tests with the real A1 signature) | Residual |
| 9 | Whoever controls the contract account can change the contract | The deployed code is a reproducible build (anyone can rebuild the same SHA-256 from the commit it names in `contract_source_metadata`), and the buyer's check refuses SAFE on any other hash. On 2026-11-10 the account's only full-access key was deleted (transaction `AgJL65MQNxGK58Ai1Forvh71YD3HwpiqLMPUL61TwvMd`): 0 access keys | Mitigated: "Account is locked (no access keys)"; the contract kept working afterwards (a payment signed and mined). Residual: no bug can be fixed in place |
| 10 | Stuck nonce or missing gas on the external chain | The nonce is assigned at signing time (cancelled proposals leave no gaps); fees are fixed when proposing, so a proposal with stale fees must be cancelled and proposed again; the cancel transaction in threat 1 helps. Transactions that can never be mined (gas limit below 21000, zero fee) are refused (review P2). A stuck nonce can be re-signed (`replace_nonce`) or cancelled (`cancel_nonce`) | Partial |
| 11 | EIP-7702 authorizations or message signatures obtained from the contract | The contract only ever builds and hashes EIP-1559 (type 2) transactions with one of five calldata shapes (see the core rule); there is no path that signs a message, a permit or an authorization | Mitigated by construction |
| 12 | Replay across chains | `chain_id` is inside the EIP-1559 hash, so a signature for one chain is invalid on another. The derivation path (`vault-<id>`) is the same for every EVM chain, so each vault has the same address everywhere; the policy must fix which chain ids a vault may sign for | Mitigated: `chain_id` is in the hash (tested against viem); the policy names one chain; the first signature fixes the vault's chain for good, also across sales (general review G2, checked live) |
| 13 | During `nft_transfer_call` the receiver owns the NFT until the transfer resolves and may hand it back: meanwhile it could set a policy with delay 0, sign a payment from the vault, and return it | Until the transfer resolves nobody can act as the vault's owner: no policy, proposal, signature, cancellation, sale, approval or transfer; the resolution gets 20 Tgas; ownership changes are recorded (`vault_transfer_info`) | Mitigated (general review H1; unit tests, negative control). Live: a receiver that tried to set a policy during the call was refused and the vault came back to the sender. Sandbox: `a_transfer_call_receiver_cannot_act_as_owner` (the only failed receipt is the vault refusing the receiver). The lock lapses after 1 hour if the resolution never ran |
| 14 | An approved account (e.g. a marketplace) moves the NFT | Every transfer path, by the owner or by an approved account, goes through the same wrapper where the locks will live; approvals are cleared on transfer and their deposit refunded | Mitigated: an approved marketplace can only transfer during an open sale window (unit tests) |
| 15 | A token id is reused, so two owners share one EVM account | Ids are sequential and never reissued; there is no burn | Tested (unit test) |
| 16 | **Stolen owner key.** A thief with the owner's NEAR key transfers the NFT to an account of theirs; the transfer clears the policy and the first policy of a locked vault applies at once, so the delay and the guardian are skipped | A transfer needs a sale window (`start_sale`) that opens after max(policy delay, 10 minutes) and that the guardian can veto (`veto_sale`) | Mitigated by M8 **when the vault has a guardian**, with residuals: every transfer needs a sale window that waits the policy delay and the guardian can veto (unit tests; vetoed live). Every sale waits at least 10 minutes, also without a policy, so an owner who still holds the key can cancel a thief's sale. Residual: a thief can replace the guardian after max(2 x delay, 3 days) (threat 18); without a guardian, nobody can veto the sale, and the new owner starts with fresh daily caps (M12, R12-4) |
| 17 | A loosening policy change applies without anyone noticing | Loosening waits the current delay; the guardian of the policy in force can veto; tightening applies at once; changes emit events | Partial: tested on testnet (veto, early apply refused, apply after delay) |
| 18 | A lost or hostile guardian freezes the vault by vetoing every proposal and its own removal | The guardian cannot veto its own replacement; replacing or removing it waits max(2 x delay, 3 days), even with delay 0; the owner can cancel that change | Mitigated (unit + testnet). Trade-off: a thief with the owner key can remove the guardian after that wait |
| 19 | The signer's answer cannot be read (format change) or never arrives, and the vault stays locked forever | The callback reads the answer by hand and never panics; unreadable or failed answers are *uncertain* (nonce kept, no reuse for another transaction, proposal pinned); the owner can abandon a signature after 1 hour; a stale answer cannot land on another signature (the callback carries the hash) | Mitigated (unit tests; uncertain path triggered live with a 0 deposit). Since M12 (R12-5) only an answer that recovers to another key is undone; any format the contract does not expect (for example recovery_id 27/28) is uncertain, because the signer may hold a valid signature (unit tests, attack suite with a mock signer in that format) |
| 20 | A failed signature is retried and paid twice | A retry after an uncertain outcome is pinned to the same nonce and produces the identical transaction, so at most one can be mined; it is not charged twice | Mitigated (unit + live) |
| 21 | The policy moves to another EVM chain after signing and the nonce counter no longer matches that chain (unminable transactions) | The first signature fixes the vault's chain; `set_policy` and `apply_policy` refuse any other chain, also for a buyer; older vaults read it from their history | Mitigated (unit tests; vault 29 refuses a mainnet policy on testnet) |

| 22 | A sale started long ago leaves the vault transferable when the owner no longer expects it, or keeps it frozen | A sale window closes 24 h after it opens and never carries over to a later owner; a closed sale no longer freezes the vault and can be replaced; the owner can cancel it | Mitigated (unit tests) |
| 23 | **Audit-to-payment gap.** The buyer audits sale N; the seller cancels it, signs a payment, keeps it and starts sale N+1; the buyer pays trusting the old audit | Sales have unique numbers. `buy(token_id, N)` pays the seller and transfers the NFT in one transaction, only during sale N; for a sale without a price, a transfer whose memo is `sale:<N>` only succeeds during sale N | Mitigated for `buy` and bound transfers (unit tests; live: a purchase and a transfer naming an older sale were refused). Residual: a payment made outside the contract and not bound to the sale |

| 24 | **A fooled or compromised agent** (prompt injection, a malicious invoice, a stolen agent key) tries to pay more, pay someone else or move NFTs | The agent only reaches `propose` and `execute_proposal`, which check the same policy as the owner's calls: daily caps, approved recipients, NFT daily counts, the delay and the guardian's veto | Mitigated by construction; tested (unit tests `the_agent_proposes_and_signs_after_the_delay`, `the_agent_waits_the_delay_too`, `the_guardian_vetoes_an_agent_proposal`; sandbox test `an_agent_spends_within_the_policy_and_the_guardian_can_veto`; attacks labelled `agent`; live on vault 78: an "ignore your rules" payment and an unknown recipient refused, duplicate invoices vetoed). Residual: within the policy the agent can still spend up to the caps on approved recipients; the guardian and the delay are the only defence there |
| 25 | **The agent escalates**: changes the policy, removes the guardian, sells or transfers the vault, cancels nonces or the owner's proposals | Every owner-only method keeps the owner check; only `propose` and `execute_proposal` accept the proposer | Mitigated (unit tests `the_agent_cannot_change_the_policy`, `the_agent_cannot_start_a_sale`, `the_agent_cannot_cancel_a_nonce`, `the_agent_cannot_cancel_the_owners_proposal`, `the_agent_cannot_transfer_the_vault`; attacks labelled `agent`). After M21: the agent cannot re-sign a nonce that was already signed (`the_agent_cannot_replace_a_signed_nonce`, R21-4) and cannot also be the guardian (`the_agent_cannot_be_the_guardian`, R21-5). It can still sign a proposal the owner queued once it is ready, which is within the policy |
| 26 | An owner, or a thief with the owner's key, quietly names an agent of their own to spend later | Naming or changing the proposer counts as a loosening: it waits the current delay and the guardian can veto it; removing it applies at once | Mitigated (unit tests `naming_an_agent_waits_the_delay_and_removing_it_is_immediate`, `naming_a_proposer_or_raising_an_nft_count_is_a_loosening`) |
| 27 | The seller's agent keeps spending after a sale | Every change of owner deletes the policy, so the agent goes with it; while a sale is open the vault signs only cancellations | Mitigated (unit test `a_sale_removes_the_agent`) |
| 28 | The agent griefs the owner: fills the queue (at most 8 proposals), keeps a signature in flight, starves the signer of its deposit, or signs a transaction with a fee too low to be mined | The owner can cancel any proposal; each proposal costs its proposer a storage deposit; a signature in flight ends with the signer's answer or a timeout. After M21: the signer deposit is never below the minimum (`a_zero_sign_deposit_is_raised_to_the_minimum`, R21-1); an identical retry of an uncertain nonce is not blocked by its own charge (`an_uncertain_retry_is_not_blocked_by_its_own_charge`, R21-1); a proposal put back after a failed signature is never dropped by a full queue (`a_full_queue_never_drops_a_proposal_after_a_failed_signature`, R21-3) | Residual: an agent can make the owner spend transactions cancelling its proposals, and (R21-2) can sign a payment with a fee too low to be mined, which blocks later nonces until the owner signs `cancel_nonce`. The owner removes the agent at once (tightening) |
| 29 | **An NFT transfer that is not what it looks like**: sent from another address the vault approved, an NFT approval (`approve`, `setApprovalForAll`), or calldata that parses differently from what the contract checked | The classifier accepts only the exact `safeTransferFrom` selectors and lengths (100 bytes for ERC-721, 196 for ERC-1155 with empty data); the `from` word must be the vault's own address; NFT approvals are never built | Mitigated (unit tests `classify_nft_transfers`, `nft_refusals`; attack labelled 3: "send an NFT the vault does not own"). M21 reviews the parsing edge cases |
| 30 | NFTs leave faster than the owner expects | Each collection has its own `daily_count`; ERC-1155 counts its amount; NFTs share the token spending window; raising a count is a loosening | Mitigated (unit tests `an_nft_sent_from_the_vault_to_an_allowed_destination_is_allowed_and_counted`, `an_erc1155_transfer_counts_its_amount`; attack labelled 6; live on vault 77: the second transfer of the day was refused at signing) |
| 31 | The upgrade that added agent mode corrupts stored policies | `migrate_policies(from, limit)` is private (only the contract account), idempotent and reads the old layout explicitly | Mitigated (unit test `stored_policies_from_before_agent_mode_are_migrated`; sandbox rehearsal `upgrade_and_migrate_policies`; done on testnet: 7 policies converted, a second pass converted 0). It is removed from risk once the contract is locked (threat 9) |

## Out of scope

Legal status of selling an account, the quality of the agent's own decisions (the contract assumes it can be wrong), front-ends other than the reference page, chains other than one EVM test network, Bitcoin (one signature per UTXO).
