# V2 design: what the next contract would change

**Status: design only. Nothing here is built or deployed.** The contract that runs today (`m3.alan-vault-2026.testnet`, reproducible build `0ff14e79…`) is locked with no access keys, so it cannot be changed; its known limits are listed in the README. This page takes each of those limits and says what a second contract would do about it, which property must then always hold (the invariant), and which tests would have to fail before the change and pass after it. A V2 would be a new contract at a new account, built reproducibly and locked the same way; vaults move to it through the normal payment path described in [MIGRATION.md](MIGRATION.md).

What V2 keeps from V1, unchanged: one signing path (`execute_proposal`), the contract building every hash itself from validated fields, every signature checked against the vault's own key, the two-phase handover, the agent's lack of power over the rules, and no admin key.

## Summary

| # | Limit today (README, Known limits) | V2 change | Invariant | Priority |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Fixed 24-hour windows: up to twice a cap can be signed across a window boundary (threat 6) | Rolling 24-hour window | In any 24 hours, what was signed never exceeds the cap | High |
| 2 | Without a guardian, the caps reset with a handover (R12-4, threats 2 and 16) | Spending follows the vault, not the owner | A change of owner never makes room under a cap | High |
| 3 | The signer is trusted to give each vault its own key (R12-14, threat 8) | Index of keys; a repeated key is refused at mint | Two vaults never hold the same key | High |
| 4 | A vault with an agent can have no guardian, or a waiting time of 0 | An agent requires a guardian and a minimum waiting time | Whatever an agent proposes can be stopped by a person | High |
| 5 | The same invoice can be paid twice if it fits the rules (seen live on vault 78) | Optional invoice reference, unique per vault | The same reference is never signed twice in its retention period | Medium |
| 6 | An agent can sign a payment with a fee too low to be mined (R21-2) | Minimum fee set by the owner for the agent | An agent never signs below the owner's floor | Medium |
| 7 | One EVM chain per vault | One nonce counter per chain | Every chain's nonces are consecutive and never reused | Medium |
| 8 | `new` has no caller check (R12-10) | Only the contract account can initialise | Nobody else chooses the signer | Low |
| 9 | Small amounts stay in the contract: no withdraw (R12-11) | Claimable refunds | Every unused deposit or unpaid price belongs to someone who can claim it | Low |
| 10 | A purchase memo must start with `sale:` (R12-13) | A structured purchase field | A transfer is bound to a sale only by an exact, typed field | Low |
| 11 | A cancellation needs room under the daily cap: with the cap used up, a stuck nonce cannot be cancelled until the window resets (found by an external review) | A small separate allowance for cancellations | The owner can always cancel a stuck nonce, and cancellations alone can burn at most a small, fixed amount of gas per day | Medium |
| 12 | The agent can sign any ready proposal, the owner's too | Each proposal records who created it; the agent signs only its own | An agent never signs a payment it did not propose | Low |

Not in this list on purpose: trust in the MPC network (threat 8's main part) and reading Ethereum (the buyer's audit) are outside what any NEAR contract can do; the guardian's alerts (M23) and warning signs (M35) are off-chain tools and can improve without a new contract.

## 1. Rolling 24-hour window

**Today.** A window opens with the first charge after the previous one expired and lasts 24 hours (`policy.rs`, `Window { start_ms, spent }`). An agent that spends the full cap just before the window ends can spend it again right after: up to twice the cap in a few minutes.

**Change.** Keep a short list of charges `(signed_ms, amount)` per asset and count only those of the last 24 hours. The list is bounded (for example 32 charges per asset in any 24 hours); a payment that would not fit is refused rather than an old charge dropped, so the bound can only make the vault stricter.

**Invariant.** For every moment t, the sum of charges with `signed_ms` in (t − 24 h, t] is at most the cap in force at t.

**Tests that would have to be written.**
- Spend the cap at 23:59 of a window, then try again at 00:01: V1 allows it, V2 must refuse it.
- A charge expires exactly 24 hours later, not one millisecond earlier.
- A property test: random sequences of times and amounts never break the invariant (checked by brute force over every 24-hour interval).
- The list never grows past its bound, and an uncertain signature keeps its charge (as today).

**Cost.** More storage per vault (a few hundred bytes) and slightly more gas per signature.

## 2. Spending follows the vault, not the owner

**Today.** A change of owner deletes the policy and its spending windows. With a guardian, the guardian can veto the handover; without one, an owner (or a thief with the owner's key) can sell the vault to themselves after the 10-minute minimum and start with fresh caps.

**Change.** Keep the spending record when the vault changes owner. The new owner's first policy starts with the previous owner's charges of the last 24 hours already counted. The policy itself is still deleted (a buyer must not inherit the seller's recipients or agent).

**Invariant.** A change of owner never makes room under a cap: what was signed in the last 24 hours still counts.

**Tests.** Spend the cap, sell to another account, set a new policy with the same cap: the next payment must be refused until the old charges expire. A buyer with a larger cap gets only the difference.

**Cost.** A buyer may receive a vault with part of today's cap already used; the web page and the buyer's audit would have to show it.

## 3. One key per vault, checked

**Today.** At mint, the contract asks the signer for the vault's key and stores it; it does not check that no other vault received the same key. Two vaults with the same key would share one Ethereum account.

**Change.** Keep an index `public key → token id`. In the mint callback, a key already in the index is refused: the vault is not created and the deposit is returned.

**Invariant.** The map from vault to key is one-to-one.

**Tests.** A mock signer that answers the same key twice: the second mint fails and refunds; a sandbox test with the real flow; storage of the index is paid by the mint deposit.

## 4. An agent always has a guardian and time to use it

**Today.** The README advises that a vault with an agent should have a guardian and a waiting time, but the contract accepts a policy with an agent, no guardian and a waiting time of 0. Then nothing stands between a fooled agent and the daily cap.

**Change.** A policy that names a `proposer` must also name a guardian (already required to be a different account) and a waiting time of at least a minimum, for example 10 minutes.

**Invariant.** Every payment an agent proposes waits at least the minimum and can be vetoed by a person.

**Tests.** `set_policy` with an agent and no guardian is refused; with an agent and a waiting time below the minimum, refused; removing the guardian while an agent is named is refused (remove the agent first).

## 5. The same invoice is not paid twice

**Today.** On vault 78 the AI agent proposed the same hosting invoice twice; both fit the rules, and only the guardian stopped the second one. The warning signs (M35) now flag it, but they only advise.

**Change.** `propose` takes an optional `reference` (for example the hash of the supplier's invoice number). The contract stores the references of signed and queued proposals for a retention period (for example 90 days) and refuses a second proposal with the same reference.

**Invariant.** Within the retention period, a reference is signed at most once per vault.

**Tests.** The same reference twice: the second proposal is refused; after a veto or a cancellation, the reference can be used again; after the retention period, it can be used again; storage is bounded and paid.

**Limit.** This stops honest mistakes and naive agents, not an attacker, who can change the reference. Against that, the cap, the approved recipients and the guardian remain the defence.

## 6. A fee floor for the agent

**Today.** An agent can sign a payment whose fee is too low to be mined; every later payment of the vault waits behind that nonce until the owner cancels it (R21-2).

**Change.** The policy gets a `min_max_fee_per_gas` that applies when the signer is the agent (the owner keeps full control of fees). `execute_proposal` by the agent refuses a proposal below it.

**Invariant.** An agent never signs a transaction below the owner's floor.

**Tests.** The agent signs below the floor: refused; the owner signs below the floor: allowed; raising the floor is a tightening (applies at once), lowering it is a loosening (waits and can be vetoed).

## 7. One nonce counter per chain

**Today.** The first signature fixes the vault's EVM chain, because there is one nonce counter.

**Change.** Store `next_nonce` per `chain_id`, and let a policy list rules per chain. The address is already the same on every EVM chain, and the chain id is already inside every signed hash.

**Invariant.** For each chain, the nonces the vault signs are consecutive and never reused; a signature for one chain is never valid on another.

**Tests.** Sign on Sepolia and on a second test chain in turn: each counter advances on its own; replaying a signed transaction on the other chain fails; the handover audit checks every chain the vault used.

**Cost.** More audit work for a buyer (one check per chain), and more ways to misconfigure a vault. This is why it is medium priority and comes after 1 to 4.

## 8 to 10. Small fixes

- **Initialisation.** `new` checks that the caller is the contract account itself, so deploying and initialising in two transactions cannot let someone else choose the signer. Test: `new` from another account is refused.
- **Refunds.** An unused signer deposit and the price of a sale whose seller account no longer exists are recorded per account and can be claimed. Test: the claim pays exactly once; the contract's balance never falls below what it owes.
- **Purchase binding.** `buy` already binds a purchase to a sale. Transfers through a marketplace would carry the sale number in a typed field of `msg` instead of a memo prefix. Test: a malformed or missing sale number never binds.

## 11. Cancellations always possible

**Today.** `cancel_nonce` charges its worst-case gas against the ETH daily cap (`check_cancel` in `policy.rs`), so it cannot be used to burn the vault's ETH in fees. But if the day's cap is used up, for example by an agent that also left a nonce stuck with a fee too low (R21-2), the owner cannot cancel until the 24-hour window resets.

**Change.** A separate cancellation allowance per day (for example the gas of 3 cancellations at the policy's max gas cost), charged instead of the main cap when the main cap has no room.

**Invariant.** With a stuck nonce, the owner can always sign a cancellation within the day; cancellations alone never cost more than that allowance per day.

**Tests.** Use up the cap, leave a stuck nonce, cancel it: V1 refuses, V2 must accept; a fourth cancellation the same day with the cap still used up is refused; the allowance resets with the window.

## 12. The agent signs only its own proposals

**Today.** The agent can sign any ready proposal, including one the owner created. It cannot change it (same recipient and amount, after the waiting time), but it can sign it before the owner meant to.

**Change.** Each proposal records `created_by`; `execute_proposal` by the agent requires `created_by == agent`. The owner can still sign any proposal.

**Invariant.** An agent never signs a payment it did not propose.

**Tests.** The owner proposes, the agent tries to execute: refused; the agent proposes and executes: allowed; the owner executes the agent's proposal: allowed.

## What V2 does not try to solve

- **Trust in Chain Signatures.** V2 still depends on NEAR's MPC network to sign; it keeps checking every answer against the vault's key.
- **An escape hatch.** An owner-triggered "move everything to my V3 vault" path beyond the daily cap was considered and left out: it would also be a thief's fastest path, and the normal path (approved recipient, waiting time, guardian veto) already works ([MIGRATION.md](MIGRATION.md)).
- **Upgradability.** V2 would be locked like V1. Being able to fix bugs in place would mean someone holds a key that can change the rules, which is what this project avoids.

## Order of work, if V2 is built

1. Changes 1 to 4, each with its failing test first, then the code, then the attack suite extended (one attack per invariant).
2. A sandbox rehearsal of the move from V1 to V2 with the MIGRATION.md procedure.
3. An independent review (by a person this time, not only another model), then a reproducible build, deployment at a new account, and the key deleted.
4. Changes 5 to 7 and 11, then 8 to 10 and 12, in later versions or together if the review time allows.
