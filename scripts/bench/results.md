# Benchmark: 128 invoices, four ways to handle them

Generated by `npm run bench` (scripts/bench/run.mjs). Dataset `bench/invoices.json` (sha256 `8070e6ed527b7d60…`): 50 legitimate bills from three approved suppliers and 78 attacks of 8 kinds. Every payment was checked read-only by the live contract `m3.alan-vault-2026.testnet` (vault 79: 0.001 ETH a day, three approved recipients). The guardian's own decision is not simulated.

| | Attacks paid or let through | ETH at risk | Legitimate bills refused or flagged |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Naive agent with no protection (pays every invoice it reads; not an AI model) | 78 of 78 | 0.28389 ETH | 0 refused |
| 2. Policy Vault contract | 38 of 78 | 0.00929 ETH | 0 refused |
| 3. Contract + warning signs for the guardian | 38 reach the guardian: 16 with a red sign, 14 with only an amber one, 8 with none | 0.00160 ETH without any sign | 8 flagged (0 red) |
| 4. Contract + careful agent (`--careful`) | 22 still proposed, 16 left to a human | 0.00715 ETH | 0 left to a human |

Signs raised on the 50 legitimate bills (false alarms): duplicate-paid: 8.

What this shows and what it does not:
- Row 1 is a deliberately naive agent (regular expressions, no AI model), the worst case the contract must survive; a real model would refuse some of these attacks on its own (on vault 78, gpt-5-mini declined the "ignore your rules" invoice but approved an unknown supplier and a duplicate). The dataset is synthetic and written by the author.
- The contract is the hard boundary: what it refuses cannot be signed, whatever the agent believes. What it lets through is, by construction, inside the rules: approved recipients, under the daily limit.
- Attacks that stay inside the rules (duplicates, inflated bills, pressure to approved suppliers) reach the guardian during the waiting time. The warning signs are advice for that decision; they never block or allow anything.
- Quiet overbilling (twice the usual amount, calm words, to an approved supplier) passes the contract and raises no sign: only a guardian who reads the amount would stop it. That kind was written on purpose to show where the signs are blind.
- Each invoice is checked against a vault that has spent nothing today, so the daily limit only stops single payments here. Within a real day it also caps the total.
- The signs are simple rules written by the author, and the attacks were written by the same author: a real attacker will avoid the obvious words. Treat the amber and red counts as an upper bound.
